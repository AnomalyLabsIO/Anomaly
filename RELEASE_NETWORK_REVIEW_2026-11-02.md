## Follow-up admission, cancellation and readiness defects

The subsequent audit found two defects that the earlier passing receipts did not cover. Admission checked the new request's deadline but did not retain every incumbent's original remaining deadline. An accepted late-consent transfer could fit alone, then become infeasible when a later request shared its bandwidth. Separately, cancellation removed a source from the pump's dictionary while retaining its entry in the physical ready queue; the zero-active early return could retain cancelled sessions indefinitely. A dictionary count of zero was insufficient cleanup evidence.

The planner now carries each active and queued owner's remaining content deadline and original queue-wait allowance, simulates the entire FIFO through completion, and preserves a rechecked target's original position. Finishing the target first cannot hide an older or following owner's missed deadline. The scheduler uses individually owned linked-list nodes, removes cancelled nodes immediately, and checks registration identity before requeueing after a callback. No deadline, receive window, rate, per-update budget, protocol or production port was increased.

Regressions cover staggered accepted/rejected arrivals, a small newcomer finishing before the affected incumbent, queued followers and nonrenewable queue promises, invalid incumbent deadlines, 1,000 cancellation cycles with and without a blocked survivor, cancellation during a send, and same-source re-registration. Linked real-session tests exercise staggered file requests and 64 create/cancel cycles. Live follow-up evidence and its limits are recorded in the validation ledger below its current-checkpoint section.

The additional impaired run exposed a distinct readiness race after transfer completion. The old prefix required a local hub even after the exact content confirmation had released Ready/AddPlayer; unordered control could overtake the ordered local spawn. The fix uses `ConnectionContentBootstrap.IsAdmitted`, including current connection/crypto generation, confirmed content, prepared scripts and facility readiness. Five linked-prefix cases (old implementation failed three), three real API-dispatch cases and three coordinator cases cover that boundary. The [second native retest](E:/Projects/AnomalyLabs/Anomaly/artifacts/network-release/admission-cleanup-followup/readiness-02-qualified.json) directly observed `anomaly:announcer:vocabulary` with `contentAdmitted=true` and `localHubAvailable=false`, then reached verified/script/native Ready. No delay, new queue, protocol change or extra native hook was introduced.

## Throughput and the coordinated transfer design

Mirror has no fixed MiB/s-per-client allowance for this message type. Batch/message maximum size is a size ceiling. At WAN latency, the old single physical reliable lane's 64-packet flight window, plus the coroutine/update/storage path, is the important bottleneck.

The shipped stack starts at MTU 1024, with candidates 1024/1164/1392/1404/1424/1432. Discovery defaults off and no stock enabling assignment was found. Fragmented packets use four channeled-header plus six fragment-header bytes. Effective runtime MTU can differ with a mod/packet layer. An optimistic fragmented payload bound is `lanes * 64 * (effectiveMTU - 10) / fullRTT`:

| Full RTT, MTU 1024 | One lane | Sixteen lanes |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 20 ms | 3.09 MiB/s | 49.51 MiB/s |
| 50 ms | 1.24 MiB/s | 19.80 MiB/s |
| 100 ms | 0.62 MiB/s | 9.90 MiB/s |

These are flight-window calculations, not measured or promised throughput. ACK/polling, encryption/framing, loss, CPU, storage and application credits reduce goodput. The fork defines `Ping = averageRTT / 2`; use **RoundTripTime**, not Ping, for this calculation. A single lane cannot plausibly move 128 MiB within 120 seconds at 100 ms even at the optimistic bound. Parallel files or larger chunks on the same lane do not create more flight slots.

The current coordinated design retains logical channels 0–4 and the normal encrypted carrier. [Bulk profile 2](E:/Projects/AnomalyLabs/Anomaly/Anomaly.Shared/Networking/BulkTransferProtocol.cs:7) adds authenticated domain/attempt/session identity; 2 MiB/128-frame receive credits; at most eight unconsumed counters per domain; four files in flight; two retries; 256 KiB prefetch per transfer; and 24 KiB owned bulk batch thresholds. Client disk/hash/import work has a single asynchronous owner with session/attempt/epoch cancellation, progress is coalesced and prepared-pack work overlaps later downloads. Final SHA verification, file ACKs, atomic asset publication, script preparation and Ready remain mandatory.

Profile 2 fixes a further many-small-message issue: released-frame totals alone let counter 1 remain delayed while 2–130 are received and credited, eventually making 1 stale in a 128-counter replay bitmap. Native packet count does not bound message count inside batches. [Contiguous consumption watermarks](E:/Projects/AnomalyLabs/Anomaly/Anomaly.Shared/Networking/BulkConsumptionTracker.cs:7) and [credit-window counter distance](E:/Projects/AnomalyLabs/Anomaly/Anomaly.Shared/Networking/BulkCreditWindow.cs:60) now bound the oldest hole; counters commit only immediately before actual enqueue, and retired-session frames are filtered before advancing current replay state. This is a protocol change, not a timeout workaround.

A separate ordinary-control replay hole was found in `FileTransferCompleteMessage`: one delayed unordered completion could be overtaken by more than 128 later completions while the other three pipeline slots cycled through tiny files. [Completion now uses ReliableOrdered](E:/Projects/AnomalyLabs/Anomaly/Anomaly.Shared/Networking/Messages/FileTransferCompleteMessage.cs:12). A [per-session pacer](E:/Projects/AnomalyLabs/Anomaly/Anomaly.Server/FileTransfer/FileCompletionPacer.cs:8) permits 128 completions/s with a four-message burst across retries, leaving room for credits/ACKs under the unchanged ordinary rate limits. Existing client ownership requires both the completion marker and all chunks, so ordered control may safely overtake other physical lanes. The 136-tiny-file sender regression fails with `RejectedStale` under the old unordered contract and passes with transport-correct ordered delivery; it does not artificially reorder an ordered receiver. Manifest remains the only ordinary unordered file-control DTO, with advancement gated by its exact consent/request transaction.

The corrected [live protocol-peer cases](E:/Projects/AnomalyLabs/Anomaly/artifacts/network-release/peer-correctness-schema5-inputs/results.json) now pass ordinary cold acquisition, cancellation followed by a fresh same-identity connection, and a held real chunk. The held chunk left domain 0 at base 0/last 8/consumed 0/released 7 with `CanSend` false for at least 250 ms before the original chunk was released and the full acquisition completed. This directly exercises real wire credits and server backpressure with application dispatch reordering; it is not simulated packet loss. Cancellation was separately counted as `ExpectedCancelled`, with main-thread cleanup proof before the replacement. None of these peers sent activation or Ready. Their shared server process and concurrent offline-build load make them correctness evidence, not native QoS or clean throughput measurements.

The [fair pump](E:/Projects/AnomalyLabs/Anomaly/Anomaly.Server/FileTransfer/BulkTransferPump.cs:1) admits at most 512 KiB/32 frames per update and checks 2 ms **between operations**. One operation/maintenance/GC can overshoot. Config defaults are 8 MiB/s per-client accounted allowance, 16 MiB/s aggregate, then 20% gameplay reserve; each chunk costs payload +25% +256 bytes. A full 16 KiB chunk costs 20,736 accounted bytes, giving an ideal default payload allowance near **5.06 MiB/s per client**. This is deliberately below the lane-window model, not a measured link guarantee.

[Admission](E:/Projects/AnomalyLabs/Anomaly/Anomaly.Server/FileTransfer/FileTransferAdmission.cs:9) caps rates by negotiated lane count, actual MTU and full RTT, with a 100 ms unmeasured fallback and additional flight-window headroom. It refreshes active/queued peers, uses fair aggregate shares and reserves verification/activation time within the remaining absolute 120-second content deadline. Queued status has a nonrenewable bounded wait; transfer inactivity starts after admission. FIFO entries are physically pruned on cancellation. Operators must configure their actual uplink budget; the scoped active-player measurements are recorded below.

## Capacity finding: account for scheduler CPU before promising a queue

The [fully cold maximum-third case](E:/Projects/AnomalyLabs/Anomaly/artifacts/network-release/server-queued-max-schema5-01/admission-failure-analysis.json) **failed**. Two actual protocol peers each acquired 267,696,453 bytes in about 88 seconds; the native third client entered a roughly 50-second bounded queue and expired with zero body bytes. The configured/link-rate forecast assumed about 10.11 MiB/s aggregate payload allowance while the loaded process delivered about 5.79 MiB/s. Shared-process peer crypto/GC/I/O contributes to this load, so this is admission correctness evidence, not independent-machine throughput. The failure demonstrated that lane/uplink capacity alone cannot justify a queue promise under the unchanged 2 ms pump allowance.

The new [scheduler-capacity estimator](E:/Projects/AnomalyLabs/Anomaly/Anomaly.Server/FileTransfer/BulkSchedulerCapacity.cs:67) measures ready full-chunk send/crypto/enqueue service cost, actual update cadence and other pump work. It models the existing discrete between-operation time check, corroborates the result only with all-ready ticks actually stopped by the CPU time limit, and retains 10% forecast headroom. Credit/ACK/storage stalls cannot masquerade as CPU capacity through whole-transfer goodput. Until sufficiently sampled, admission uses no more than one default client's accounted allowance. [Initial and queued planning](E:/Projects/AnomalyLabs/Anomaly/Anomaly.Server/FileTransfer/FileTransferSession.cs:250) cap the configured aggregate by this estimate. ## Baseline: large file transfer limits

### Current limits and throughput model

Files use 16 KiB chunks on Mirror channel 4, mapped to LiteNetLib ReliableUnordered channel number 4. Its internal wire lane ID is 16. Every peer/lane has a 64-packet flight window; each fragment consumes one slot. The inspected client native constructor and current managed server agree.

Initial MTU is 1024; the largest configured candidate is 1432. Discovery defaults off, with no enabling assignment found in the inspected transport/Anomaly configuration. Active runtime MTU remains a measurement requirement. The shipped Loader scenes configure six transport entries and a 5 ms update interval, overriding the constructor's five entries and 15 ms default.

An optimistic fragmented-payload ceiling is `64 * (MTU - 10) / RTT`:

| RTT | Ceiling at MTU 1024 | Ceiling at MTU 1432 | Minimum time for 128 MiB total at MTU 1024 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 20 ms | 3.09 MiB/s | 4.34 MiB/s | 41.4 s |
| 50 ms | 1.24 MiB/s | 1.74 MiB/s | 103.4 s |
| 100 ms | 0.62 MiB/s | 0.87 MiB/s | 206.8 s |

These are calculated bounds, not measured speeds. They exclude ACK scheduling, loss/retransmission, Mirror/encryption overhead, frame scheduling, and storage. The 128 MiB example is aggregate content across files: current limits are 64 MiB/file and 256 MiB/manifest.

The sender yields for 5 ms after each chunk through a game coroutine. At 60 coroutine updates/second, one 16 KiB chunk per update is at most 0.9375 MiB/s, or about 136.5 seconds for 128 MiB. Sixty updates/second is an illustrative assumption, not a measured server rate. Removing that delay does not remove the transport-window limit.

### Recommended implementation boundary

Use a versioned, negotiated bulk profile over a small bounded pool of additional reliable channels. Configure the supported pool on both endpoints before peer construction; activate only the agreed subset after authentication. Validate the existing mapping rather than claiming globally reserved channel numbers. Keep bulk use scoped to the content acquisition transaction. Separate physical routing from authenticated logical domains explicitly; if logical channel 4 is retained beneath a physical-lane pool, its replay/reordering bounds still need review rather than assuming extra enum members are required or sufficient.

Select lane count from the required bytes-in-flight at the target RTT, subject to global capacity. Eight additional stock windows have an optimistic combined capacity near 4.95 MiB/s at 100 ms RTT and MTU 1024; sixteen near 9.90 MiB/s. These figures are capacity models, not throughput promises or permission for unbounded upload. At an achieved 4 MiB/s, 128 MiB takes 32 seconds to transfer; validation and activation add time.

Implement the profile together with:

1. Per-client byte credits covering queued and in-flight application content, plus a global upload and main-thread processing budget with fair service among joiners.
2. Small bounded Mirror bulk batches, limited outstanding envelopes, and a bounded bulk receive handoff that preserves timely normal/control processing.
3. Bounded asynchronous reads/writes/hash verification, coalesced progress reporting, and per-file/pack preparation overlapping later transfers.
4. Explicit authenticated bulk domains, compatible replay windows, registered contracts, pre-player allowlists, and separate bounded bulk rate admission.
5. Session/generation cancellation, rejection of stale credits, final hash/commit ACKs, and the existing final content/script readiness barrier.
6. Bounded admission queuing and phase-appropriate timeouts. Extending the overall deadline alone is not a performance fix.

The transport LateUpdate currently drains all queued events without a count/time budget. Sender pacing and receiver credits must prevent backlog bursts from monopolizing shared handling; expensive work belongs outside that drain. Do not simply truncate unrelated lifecycle/control processing. [Transport event drain](E:/Tools/SCPSL-14.2.7-Server/ExportedProject/Assets/Scripts/Assembly-CSharp/Mirror/LiteNetLib4Mirror/LiteNetLib4MirrorTransport.cs:116).

Increasing the native reliable window instead requires coordinated pending/receive arrays and ACK sizes on both endpoints; ProcessAck rejects mismatched ACK sizes. The IL2CPP constructor hardcodes 64 and is not a public tuning API. The additional-channel design uses existing transport capabilities and is the preferred direction, conditional on the compatibility requirements above. Parallel files on the same channel, larger chunks, and warm-cache reuse do not increase that channel's flight window.
